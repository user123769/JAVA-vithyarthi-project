Problem Statement-In years household electricity consumption has increased significantly due to the rapid proliferation of electrical appliances. However individual consumers often struggle to monitor, predict and control their utility expenses. Traditional billing models rely on tiered "slab" tariff structures where the cost per unit increases as total consumption crosses specific thresholds alongside additional fixed service charges and taxes. Because monthly energy bills are delivered after the consumption cycle ends users lack real-time visibility into how specific appliances contribute to their costs. Consequently consumers face financial burdens and fail to identify energy-inefficient habits.To address this challenge there is a need, for an automated modular Electricity Bill Estimator & Consumption Analyzer. The proposed system enables users to register household appliances with their wattage ratings and daily operational hours. By converting device parameters into monthly kilowatt-hour metrics and applying customizable slab-based pricing algorithms the application generates real-time accurate billing estimates. This project empowers consumers to analyze appliance-level energy breakdowns, forecast expenses and adopt proactive energy-saving practices.

Scope of the project- The Electricity Bill Estimator project is designed to create a Java application that helps users track and manage their household energy use. The main goal is to let users input details about their devices, such as power ratings and how hours they run each day. From this information the system calculates daily and energy usage in kilowatt-hours (
kWh
).The application supports CRUD operations—create, read, update and delete—for managing appliances. Users can add devices change existing ones or remove them as needed. Behind the scenes an automated calculation engine processes the data to estimate energy consumption over a day and over a 30-day month.To get a bill amount the system uses a dynamic tariff algorithm. This accounts for tiered pricing structures, where rates change depending on how much electricity's used. It also includes fixed service fees and taxes. The final monthly bill estimate reflects real-world billing practices.Input validation is built into the system to make sure only sensible values are accepted. For example it prevents users from entering wattage or usage times longer than 24 hours in a day. This ensures results and avoids errors in calculations.While the current version focuses on user input and internal calculations future enhancements could include connecting with meters or IoT sensors for automatic data collection. Other possible features, like syncing with utility company systems or letting users pay their bills online are not part of the current scope but could be added later.

Target Users- The Electricity Bill Estimator is made for people who use electricity at home or in businesses. This includes homeowners, renters and small business owners who want to keep an eye on how power they use. The tool helps them predict bills and find ways to cut costs. It’s especially useful for those who're careful with money and want to avoid surprise charges, from higher electricity rates that kick in when usage goes up. Users can see which appliances use the energy and make better choices to reduce their monthly utility bills.

High-level Features- The application focuses on managing home appliances calculating costs automatically and analyzing energy use. The system gives users control to add, change and remove household devices and to enter the wattage they use and how many hours they run each day. At the heart of the software is a system that uses levels of pricing to turn the details of each appliance into daily and monthly energy usage, in kilowatt-hours. This system also adds rates, fixed fees and taxes to give accurate estimates of what the electricity bill will be. The application also checks the data to make sure it makes sense and has tools that show users a summary of which appliances use the energy.
